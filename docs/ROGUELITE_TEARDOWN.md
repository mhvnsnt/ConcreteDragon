# ROGUELITE TEARDOWN — Brotato + Hades (owner's retention gold standard)

Owner directive 2026-10-06: research these two deeply — "what keeps people playing" — and adapt their systems ADDITIVELY into Concrete Dragon (3D beat-em-up, infinite missions, infinite bosses, infinite unlockable characters). Base combat feel never changes; everything here orbits it.

Sources: Brotato (Blobfish, Steam app 1942280) via Steam community guides/discussions, talkandroid guide, nichegamer/screenrant/medium reviews, Wikipedia; Hades (Supergiant) via GameSpot, Inverse, Polygon, RPGSite, TheGamer, Game Informer interviews with Greg Kasavin and team. Quotes are brief and attributed; community insights are labeled as such.

## PART 1 — BROTATO

### 1.1 Core loop
20 waves, each 20–90 seconds. Survive the wave → collect materials/XP → shop phase (buy weapons/items, pick level-up stats, reroll, recycle) → next wave. A full run is under 30 minutes. Enemies scale in number and type; wave 20 is a boss. After wave 20, endless mode continues with enemies outscaling the player until death.

**Why it retains:** the loop is a slot machine of decisions — every 60–90 seconds you get a meaningful shopping/build choice, then immediately test it. The run is short enough that death never feels expensive ("one more run" costs 20 minutes max). Community consensus: "the constant itch of doing just one more run after each victory or defeat" (nichegamer review).

**Concrete Dragon adaptation:** missions ARE runs. Each mission = survive the block (timed waves or clear-the-street), then a between-mission **corner store** phase: spend cash on gear, pick stat boosts, reroll the stock, recycle unwanted gear. Keep missions short (2–4 minutes) so death is cheap and "one more mission" is always tempting.

### 1.2 Weapons: 6 slots, 4 tiers, merge, tags
- Up to 6 weapons auto-fire simultaneously. Four rarity tiers: white/blue/purple/orange.
- Two identical weapons merge into one higher-tier weapon (frees a slot, more damage). Community rule of thumb: two tier-1s often beat one tier-2 early — merging is a decision, not an auto-pilot.
- Weapons carry **tags/sets** (e.g. Explosive, Precise, Medical). The shop is weighted to offer weapons matching what you already own (~20% exact-match chance, higher for owned sets early), so builds cohere instead of scattering.
- Some characters break the mold: one can only carry 1 weapon, one carries 12 with penalties, one can't heal at all.

**Why it retains:** build identity. Tags turn a pile of weapons into a *build* — "my explosive Glutton run" vs "my dodge Ghost run." The shop weighting is the quiet genius: RNG still surprises you, but the game conspires to make your choices matter.

**Concrete Dragon adaptation — GEAR with tags:** give fighters 4–6 gear slots (gloves, wraps, chains, boots, jacket, charm). Gear pieces have tags (BRAWLER, STREET, HEAVY, SWIFT, LOUD). Duplicate gear merges to higher tiers. The corner-store stock weights toward tags you already run, so builds cohere. Fighter-specific quirks: e.g. BRICK can equip 2 heavy gear with no penalty; GHOST gets dodge-scaling gear. All additive — the tap/dodge/jump core never changes.

### 1.3 Stats: the real game
Max HP, HP regen, lifesteal, % damage, melee/ranged/elemental damage, attack speed, crit chance, engineering (turret power), range, armor (each point ≈ +6.7% effective HP), dodge %, move speed, luck (better shop/loot), harvesting (+5% XP/materials per proc). Trade-offs are explicit: red-tier items carry real drawbacks; characters start with handicaps (e.g. -100% lifesteal) that define their playstyle.

**Why it retains:** stats are a second build layer on top of weapons. Choosing between +armor and +dodge is a *philosophy* choice (tank vs avoid), and the game teaches it through play, not tutorials.

**Concrete Dragon adaptation:** port the stat vocabulary 1:1 onto gear and level-up picks: TOUGH (armor), REGEN, LIFESTEAL, CRIT, DODGE, SPEED, LUCK (better corner-store stock + pickup drops), HUSTLE (harvesting = +cash/XP). Keep POWER as % damage. Offer them as between-mission level-up choices (pick 1 of 3), exactly like Brotato's level-ups.

### 1.4 Characters: 40+, each a rules change
Dozens of unlockable potatoes, each with stat modifiers and a twist (can't heal / explodes when grabbing fruit at full HP / -100% damage but XP from live enemies). Unlock conditions are simple and shown up front. Beating Danger 5 with a character turns their select portrait gold — the prestige chase.

**Why it retains:** each character is a new game. The Medium Danger-5 review nails it: finishing every character on Danger 5 "puts you in an exclusive club, a club whose character select screen is entirely made of gold." Completionists get a visible trophy wall; everyone else gets 40+ fresh starts.

**Concrete Dragon adaptation:** the roster already unlocks via boss defeats — add per-fighter **quirk modifiers** (not just stats): e.g. unlocked brawler "RUST" deals more damage below 50% HP but can't use health pickups; "DUST" gains luck from near-misses. Add the gold-portrait prestige: beat the highest REP tier with a fighter → gold card. Visible, braggable, zero power.

### 1.5 Shop economy
Materials (from crates/trees/enemies) + gold. Between waves: buy up to 4 items/weapons, reroll the stock for a fee, recycle unwanted gear for partial refund, pick level-up stat boosts. Luck stat improves shop rarity odds.

**Why it retains:** the shop is where the run is won or lost — it's the strategy phase between action phases, and reroll/recycle give the player agency over RNG instead of victimhood.

**Concrete Dragon adaptation:** the corner store between missions, with cash earned in-mission, reroll for a fee, recycle gear for partial refund, LUCK stat improving stock. Already have cash + shop bones — this completes the loop.

### 1.6 Danger levels 0–5
Beat the run on Danger N to unlock Danger N+1 (up to 5). More enemies, new types, elites, extra bosses. Achievements are earnable on Danger 0 (the "inclusivity philosophy" per Steam community), but the gold portraits and the real prestige live at Danger 5.

**Why it retains:** a difficulty ladder with *status* at the top. Danger 5 isn't just harder — it's an identity.

**Concrete Dragon adaptation — REP TIERS:** after clearing the Street Circuit, unlock REP 1–5. Same missions, meaner streets: more enemies, elites, extra bosses, better payouts. Per-fighter gold cards for clearing top REP. (Maps cleanly onto the existing 5 difficulties — REP is the post-game ladder above BRUTAL.)

## PART 2 — HADES (Supergiant)

### 2.1 Boons: the build lottery
Gods grant boons that modify your attack, special, dash, cast, or call. Four rarities: Common/Rare/Epic/Heroic. **Duo boons** (two specific gods + prerequisites) and **Legendary boons** create chase combos. Poms of Power upgrade boon levels. Your first boon steers the whole run — "depending on the boon I get first, it sends me toward a different loadout" (Polygon). God-trial rooms offer two boons at the cost of fighting the jealous god's hazards.

**Why it retains:** every run's first god choice is a fork in the road — the build writes itself differently each time, but *you* steer it. Duo boons are the jackpot stories players tell each other.

**Concrete Dragon adaptation — BLESSINGS:** between-mission "blessings" from street figures (e.g. the CORNER PROPHET, the GYM COACH, the BLOCK ELDER — street-mythology figures, never invented canon), each modifying punch/special/dodge/jump. Rarities: COMMON/RARE/EPIC/LEGENDARY. **DUO blessings** when you hold blessings from two specific figures (e.g. PROPHET+COACH = "Sunday Service": counters trigger shockwaves). Offered as pickup choices mid-mission (pick 1 of 3) and in the corner store.

### 2.2 Weapons + Aspects
6 weapons, each with 4 unlockable Aspects (Titan Blood) that radically change playstyle. Heat rewards are tracked per weapon — pushing players to master all six, not one-trick.

**Why it retains:** aspects make one weapon feel like four. Per-weapon heat tracking turns weapon variety into a completion checklist.

**Concrete Dragon adaptation:** fighters are the "weapons"; give each fighter 2–3 **STANCES** (aspect-equivalents) unlocked with earned materials — e.g. KID BLUE: "Southpaw" (counters hit harder) vs "Slugger" (heavies are faster). Track REP-tier clears per fighter to push roster variety.

### 2.3 Mirror of Night (meta-progression)
Persistent upgrades bought with Darkness: death defiances, move speed, backstab damage, etc. Respecable. Progress is visible and permanent — every run feeds it, win or lose.

**Why it retains:** no run is ever wasted. Even a 3-minute death buys something permanent.

**Concrete Dragon adaptation — THE GYM:** a persistent upgrade board (already have POWER/TOUGH/HUSTLE — expand to ~12 nodes: extra dodge charge, pickup magnet radius, revive once per circuit, etc.). Every mission pays into it, win or lose. Respecable for cash.

### 2.4 Keepsakes
Equip one before a run: guarantees the next god you meet, gives a call ability, etc. Earned through relationships (nectar/ambrosia). A pre-run strategic choice that shapes RNG in your favor.

**Why it retains:** agency over randomness. You can't control the boon lottery, but you can load the dice.

**Concrete Dragon adaptation — CHARMS:** equip one charm before a mission: "Brass Knuckles" (first blessing is always HEAVY-tagged), "Lucky Dice" (+luck for the mission), "Second Wind" (revive once at 1 HP). Earned by beating bosses / maxing fighter affinity. One slot, pre-mission choice.

### 2.5 Pact of Punishment / Heat
Unlocked after the first clear. Stackable modifiers (enemies +20% damage per rank, new enemy abilities, hazards, restrictions), each worth Heat points. Rewards (Titan Blood, Diamonds, Ambrosia) are re-earnable per Heat level **per weapon** — raise heat one point at a time to collect every tier's rewards. RPGSite: "playing levels over and over again is a huge part of the fun of Hades — and that makes the Pact of Punishment and carefully managing your Heat level a huge part of the experience."

**Why it retains:** the endgame IS the difficulty menu. Each heat point is a new reason to replay content you've already beaten, with rewards gated per tier so no optimal path skips the climb.

**Concrete Dragon adaptation:** fold into REP TIERS (from 1.6): each REP tier = a set of stackable **STREET CONDITIONS** (e.g. "RUSH HOUR" +enemy count, "HEAVY HITTERS" +enemy damage, "BLACKOUT" limited visibility, "NO PICKUPS"). Rewards re-earn per tier per fighter — climb one tier at a time, collect everything. The wildness modifiers already in the build ARE the seed of this.

### 2.6 Narrative-through-death (the masterstroke)
Kasavin (Inverse): "Our big focus from the start with Hades was how to take the sting of failure and reduce that as much as possible, knowing that it inherently feels bad to die in a game. Even the narrative perspective of the game exists in service of that goal." Death returns you to the House of Hades, where characters comment on your run, advance their stories, and mock or encourage you. Dying is *content*. Plus God Mode: an opt-in damage-reduction boon that grows with each death (20% → 80%) — accessibility without shame.

**Why it retains:** Hades solved the roguelike's core wound — dying feels bad. Here, dying moves the story forward, so players *want* to see what happens next even when they lose.

**Concrete Dragon adaptation:** death = content. When you fall: the block reacts — rivals taunt you by name, your corner crew drags you up, and **The Narrator appears (scarce, story-moment only)** to mark the defeat as plot. Track defeat-streaks per boss with escalating dialogue. Add an opt-in **COMEBACK** toggle (God-Mode equivalent): each loss grants stacking damage reduction, clearly labeled as a training aid, no shame, no leaderboard impact.

### 2.7 Run structure & shops
4 biomes, chamber-by-chamber choices (door icons preview rewards: boon/hammer/gold/health/shop). Charon's shops mid-run, wells of Charon, mini-bosses. Every door is a decision.

**Why it retains:** the run is a branching path of visible choices — you always know what you're walking toward.

**Concrete Dragon adaptation:** mission select shows the circuit as a branching map: each node previews its reward type (blessing / gear / cash / boss). Between-mission corner store + mid-mission **BODEGA** stops (pay cash mid-run for a heal or one blessing). The Street Circuit card already exists — give it the door-preview treatment.
