# CONCRETE DRAGON — MECHANICS HARVEST (2026-10-06+)

Owner directive: research HUNDREDS of the most-loved AND most-underrated games across
3D platformers, fighters, and beat-em-ups — harvest mechanics that players love,
and fit them into Concrete Dragon **ADDITIVELY**. Never change the base game's
combat feel or core loop (tap-to-punch, counter on telegraph, KO slow-mo,
Street Thug / Big Rico naming style, style-not-power).

Effort key (one builder): S < 1 day · M 1–3 days · L 3–7 days · XL 1–2 weeks.

Each entry: mechanic name, source game, why players love it, additive fit, effort.
Games covered so far: 0 (waves below).

## WAVE 1 — BEAT-EM-UPS (the home genre)

### Combo keep-alive timer — *Streets of Rage 4* (2020)
- **Why players love it:** the combo counter only keeps climbing if you land hits
  before a short timer expires — it turns every fight into a rhythm game and
  makes players chase "one more hit" instead of playing safe.
- **Additive fit:** layer onto the existing combo counter: combo keeps building
  only while hits land within ~2s; show the timer as a shrinking ring around the
  counter. Zero change to combat itself.
- **Effort:** S

### Blitz move (double-tap-forward + attack) — *Streets of Rage 4* (2020)
- **Why players love it:** a lunging special attack that closes distance — every
  character's blitz feels like THEIR move, and it solves the "enemy just out of
  reach" frustration.
- **Additive fit:** forward-flick on the joystick + punch = character-specific
  lunging strike. New move, same buttons.
- **Effort:** M

### Star move (super, one per life/stage) — *Streets of Rage 4* (2020)
- **Why players love it:** a screen-clearing super you earn rarely — scarcity
  makes it an event, and picking the moment is a skill.
- **Additive fit:** special meter (already approved) fills slowly; at full, a
  dedicated button unleashes a cinematic star move. Additive super layer.
- **Effort:** M

### Desperation attack (costs health) — *Final Fight* (1989)
- **Why players love it:** spinning 360° attack that costs a chunk of your own
  HP — the ultimate "get off me" panic button with a real price, so using it
  feels brave, not cheap.
- **Additive fit:** double-tap dodge button (or dedicated gesture) = 360° spin
  costing 10% current HP. New option, no core change.
- **Effort:** S

### Knife/weapon pickup with limited swings — *Final Fight* (1989)
- **Why players love it:** grabbing a knife off a thug flips the power dynamic
  for 10 seconds — weapons feel precious because they break.
- **Additive fit:** weapon pickups already approved; Final Fight's lesson is
  scarcity + big damage per swing. Fold into the weapon system spec.
- **Effort:** S (design note)

### Mixtape stance system (two switchable loadouts) — *Double Dragon Neon* (2012)
- **Why players love it:** two "mixtapes" = two stances you swap between
  mid-fight (e.g. power stance vs speed stance), effectively doubling your
  moveset without doubling buttons.
- **Additive fit:** unlockable second stance per fighter (e.g. Kid Blue swaps
  balanced → rushdown). Pure addition to move variety.
- **Effort:** L

### High-five revive — *Double Dragon Neon* (2012)
- **Why players love it:** in co-op, reviving your partner is a HIGH-FIVE —
  the mechanic IS the vibe. (Relevant if co-op ever lands.)
- **Additive fit:** park for co-op lane; note as identity reference.
- **Effort:** XL (co-op dependent)

### Taunt to build super meter — *TMNT: Shredder's Revenge* (2022)
- **Why players love it:** taunting mid-fight is disrespect AS gameplay — you
  risk getting hit to charge your super faster. Style and greed in one button.
- **Additive fit:** taunt button/gesture charges special meter faster but leaves
  you open. Adds swagger with zero combat change.
- **Effort:** S

### Radical mode (full meter = temporary powered state) — *TMNT: Shredder's Revenge* (2022)
- **Why players love it:** filling the meter doesn't just unlock a super — you
  can instead enter a timed powered-up state. Two ways to spend the same
  resource = decisions.
- **Additive fit:** special meter at full: tap for super, or hold for 8s
  powered state (faster, glowing). Same meter, more expression.
- **Effort:** M

### Buyable moves + food healing + stats — *River City Ransom* (1989)
- **Why players love it:** it's a brawler with RPG guts — earn cash, buy
  Dragon Feet at the dojo, eat sushi to heal and raise stats. Every run makes
  your fighter permanently more YOURS.
- **Additive fit:** dojo shop between missions: spend cash on new moves
  (not stats — style-not-power keeps stats out of the shop). Food pickups heal.
  The cash economy already exists.
- **Effort:** M

### Coins fly out, shops between levels — *Scott Pilgrim vs. The World* (2010)
- **Why players love it:** enemies burst into coins you physically grab, then
  you spend them in shops between stages — the loop of earn → browse → power
  up is addictive on its own.
- **Additive fit:** cash already drops; add between-mission shop screens
  (moves, skins, consumables). The upgrade shop exists — extend it.
- **Effort:** M

### Parry (tap forward on impact) — *Fight'N Rage* (2017)
- **Why players love it:** a perfectly-timed forward tap negates damage and
  staggers the attacker — the highest-skill defensive option in any brawler,
  and it feels incredible.
- **Additive fit:** alongside dodge: forward-tap within the counter window =
  parry (no damage, enemy staggered, counter window opens). Adds depth to the
  existing telegraph system.
- **Effort:** M

### Rage meter (damage taken → damage dealt) — *The TakeOver* (2019)
- **Why players love it:** getting beaten up charges a meter you unleash for
  massive damage — the comeback fantasy, mechanized.
- **Additive fit:** separate small "GRIT" meter fills when hit; at full, next
  special is doubled. Comeback layer, no core change.
- **Effort:** S

### Three-plane movement — *Guardian Heroes* (1996)
- **Why players love it:** three depth lanes you hop between — positioning
  becomes chess, and it makes one screen feel three times bigger.
- **Additive fit:** the 3D build already has depth; formalize 3 lanes with a
  lane-hop on dodge. Structures the depth players already move in.
- **Effort:** M

### Branching stage paths — *Guardian Heroes* (1996) / *D&D: Shadow over Mystara* (1996)
- **Why players love it:** choices mid-stage (which door, which route) change
  the level — replayability without new content, and YOUR run feels authored.
- **Additive fit:** mission forks: after a wave, pick one of two routes
  (different enemies/pickups/boss). Data-driven, huge replay value.
- **Effort:** L

### Animal orb companions — *Castle Crashers* (2008)
- **Why players love it:** little creatures follow you and grant perks (find
  hidden items, breathe fire) — they're collectibles with personality.
- **Additive fit:** unlockable corner-crew mascots (street pigeons? stray
  dogs?) granting small perks (cash magnet, pickup radar). Pure charm layer.
- **Effort:** M

### Berserk pickup (risk/reward power) — *Mother Russia Bleeds* (2016)
- **Why players love it:** a syringe that makes you a monster but drains you —
  power with a price tag creates stories.
- **Additive fit:** "ADRENALINE" pickup: 10s of double damage, then brief
  exhaustion. Reskinned to street (no drugs). Risk/reward pickup class.
- **Effort:** S

### Ki meter + super attacks + flight segments — *Dragon Ball GT: Transformation* (GBA, 2005)
- **Why players love it:** the GBA game the owner named — a meter that builds
  as you fight, spent on screen-shaking supers, with flying chase segments
  breaking up the brawling.
- **Additive fit:** special meter already approved; add "sky chase" bonus
  missions (auto-scrolling, dodge + blast). Honors the reference directly.
- **Effort:** L

### Command your crew — *The Warriors* (Rockstar, 2005)
- **Why players love it:** you lead a gang — order them to follow, hold, or
  wreck everything. The fantasy isn't one fighter, it's YOUR crew.
- **Additive fit:** unlockable crew backup: call 1–2 AI allies for one wave
  (cooldown). Gang fantasy without co-op netcode.
- **Effort:** L

### Rage mode + mugging minigame — *The Warriors* (2005)
- **Why players love it:** filling rage unlocks a berserk mode; mugging
  pedestrians for cash makes the street feel like YOUR territory.
- **Additive fit:** rage = the GRIT meter above; mugging = shake down
  defeated enemies for bonus cash (hold near a downed enemy). Street flavor.
- **Effort:** M

### HEAT actions (contextual finishers) — *Yakuza / Like a Dragon* (2005+)
- **Why players love it:** near a wall/bike/sign with full HEAT, you get a
  brutal contextual finisher — the environment becomes your moveset, and
  players hunt for setups.
- **Additive fit:** near destructibles/walls with full special meter: prompt
  for HEAT finisher using the scenery. Turns props into finishers.
- **Effort:** L

### Multiple fighting styles per character — *Yakuza* (2005+)
- **Why players love it:** switching styles (rush/brawler/beast) mid-fight
  keeps one character fresh for 50 hours.
- **Additive fit:** same as Neon mixtapes above — converges; implement once.
- **Effort:** L (shared)

### Environmental takedowns — *Sleeping Dogs* (2012)
- **Why players love it:** grapple an enemy to a phone booth, dumpster, or
  AC unit for an instant kill — every prop is a loaded weapon.
- **Additive fit:** grapple (approved direction) + nearby prop = environmental
  KO prompt. Builds on destructibles + grapple systems.
- **Effort:** L

### Style-based movesets + crowd shove — *Def Jam: Fight for NY* (2004)
- **Why players love it:** pick from 5 martial arts styles per fighter, and
  the crowd literally throws you back into the fight — the audience
  participates.
- **Additive fit:** style system converges with mixtapes; crowd-shove fits the
  owner's conditional-crowd rule (some missions): knocked toward the crowd,
  they shove you back with a damage bounce.
- **Effort:** M

### Custom combo roulette — *God Hand* (2006)
- **Why players love it:** assign ANY unlocked move to the 4 face buttons —
  your combo string is literally custom-built. No two players fight alike.
- **Additive fit:** move loadout screen: slot unlocked moves to
  punch/kick/heavy/special. The deepest additive system on this list.
- **Effort:** L

### Adaptive difficulty (the Level Die) — *God Hand* (2006)
- **Why players love it:** the game watches you — dominate and it silently
  cranks up (better rewards); struggle and it eases off. No menu, no shame.
- **Additive fit:** silent dynamic difficulty layered UNDER the 4–5 manual
  levels: within Normal, nudge enemy aggression ±15% by player performance.
  Invisible kindness.
- **Effort:** M

### 360° dodge + pummel finishers — *God Hand* (2006)
- **Why players love it:** the dodge is a full sidestep dance, and downed
  enemies can be pummeled — disrespect as a mechanic.
- **Additive fit:** dodge already approved; add pummel prompt on downed
  enemies (mash for bonus cash/style). Converges with ground-and-pound.
- **Effort:** S

## WAVE 2 — FIGHTERS (1v1 depth, stolen for brawler combat)

### Motion-input special moves — *Street Fighter II* (1991)
- **Why players love it:** quarter-circle + punch = fireball. The input IS the
  ritual — execution skill separates players and makes every special feel earned.
- **Additive fit:** touch-friendly motion inputs: swipe-forward + punch =
  fighter's projectile/rush special. Execution depth on mobile.
- **Effort:** M

### Parry (tap toward on impact) — *Street Fighter III: 3rd Strike* (1999)
- **Why players love it:** the Daigo moment — parrying Chun-Li's entire super
  is the most famous fighting game clip ever. Perfect defense as pure hype.
- **Additive fit:** converges with Fight'N Rage parry above — implement once,
  tune for brawler crowds.
- **Effort:** M (shared)

### Focus attack (absorb one hit, crumple) — *Street Fighter IV* (2008)
- **Why players love it:** hold to absorb a hit with armor, release for a
  crumple — offense and defense in one button, and the mind games are endless.
- **Additive fit:** hold heavy = armored charge attack; release timing sets
  damage. New heavy variant, same button.
- **Effort:** M

### Drive system (one resource, many options) — *Street Fighter 6* (2023)
- **Why players love it:** a single Drive meter fuels parry, rush, reversal,
  and armored attacks — one resource, five decisions. Elegant depth.
- **Additive fit:** merge special/grit meters into one DRIVE-style meter with
  multiple spends (super, parry-cancel, burst movement). Simplifies UI too.
- **Effort:** L

### Modern controls (one-button specials) — *Street Fighter 6* (2023)
- **Why players love it:** accessibility that doesn't insult veterans — new
  players do cool moves instantly, pros still optimize.
- **Additive fit:** "ASSIST" toggle in settings: special button does the
  optimal special automatically. Accessibility = the owner's humanity rule.
- **Effort:** S

### 3v3 team elimination — *The King of Fighters* (1994+)
- **Why players love it:** your team of 3 vs theirs, elimination order — team
  composition and order strategy before a punch is thrown.
- **Additive fit:** "CREW BATTLE" mode: pick 3 unlocked fighters, elimination
  rules. Uses the unlockable roster directly.
- **Effort:** M

### Hops, rolls, Just Defend — *KOF / Garou: Mark of the Wolves* (1999)
- **Why players love it:** short hops beat lows, rolls beat throws, Just Defend
  (block at the last frame) refunds health and meter — defense with teeth.
- **Additive fit:** hop (up-flick) over sweeps; roll through enemies;
  last-frame block = Just Defend (health back + meter). Defensive toolkit.
- **Effort:** M

### TOP system (comeback zone at low HP) — *Garou: Mark of the Wolves* (1999)
- **Why players love it:** pick a HP range where you deal bonus damage — when
  you're bleeding, you're DANGEROUS. Comebacks are designed in.
- **Additive fit:** "LAST STAND": below 30% HP, damage +25% and screen edges
  burn. The comeback fantasy, always on.
- **Effort:** S

### Plane switching (dodge into background) — *Fatal Fury* (1991)
- **Why players love it:** hop between foreground/background planes to dodge —
  the arena has depth you can weaponize.
- **Additive fit:** converges with Guardian Heroes 3-plane above — one system.
- **Effort:** M (shared)

### Roman Cancel (cancel anything for meter) — *Guilty Gear* (1998+)
- **Why players love it:** spend meter to cancel any move's recovery — combos
  become jazz improvisation, and the yellow/purple/red variants add layers.
- **Additive fit:** "FLOW CANCEL": spend half special meter to cancel recovery
  into dodge or another attack. High-level expression tool.
- **Effort:** M

### Burst (combo breaker with risk) — *Guilty Gear / BlazBlue*
- **Why players love it:** one per round, break any combo — but whiff it and
  you're punished. The panic button with a brain.
- **Additive fit:** once-per-mission BURST: breaks enemy combo/grab, punishable
  if baited. Bosses can have it too (fair warning telegraph).
- **Effort:** M

### Dust launcher (universal overhead launcher) — *Guilty Gear* (1998+)
- **Why players love it:** one universal launcher everyone shares — the
  combo system has a common language.
- **Additive fit:** down+heavy = universal launcher across all fighters.
  Standardizes juggle starters.
- **Effort:** S

### Custom assists — *Skullgirls* (2012)
- **Why players love it:** pick ANY move as your teammate's assist — team
  building is lab work, and no two teams play alike.
- **Additive fit:** with crew backup (Warriors entry): choose your backup
  fighter's assist move. Team expression.
- **Effort:** M

### Anti-infinite (Undizzy/IPS) — *Skullgirls* (2012)
- **Why players love it:** the game detects repeated combo loops and bursts
  them — infinites are impossible BY DESIGN. Fairness as a feature.
- **Additive fit:** combo system caps: same move 3x in one juggle = auto-drop
  with a "READ!" popup. Keeps juggles honest.
- **Effort:** S

### Fatalities / finishers — *Mortal Kombat* (1992+)
- **Why players love it:** the reason MK survived 30 years — winning isn't
  enough, you get to STYLE on them. Finishers are the reward for dominance.
- **Additive fit:** FINISHER prompt on final boss wave kills (input sequence
  → cinematic KO). The owner's slow-mo law makes these the ultimate
  big-moment. Style, never power.
- **Effort:** M

### Test Your Might (mash minigame) — *Mortal Kombat* (1992+)
- **Why players love it:** button-mashing to break boards/bars — a pure
  physical mini-game break between fights.
- **Additive fit:** destructible "MIGHT" objects: mash to smash for bonus
  cash. Between-wave breather.
- **Effort:** S

### Juggles + wall combos + Rage Art — *Tekken* (1994+)
- **Why players love it:** launching someone and carrying them to the wall is
  Tekken's whole language; Rage Art = comeback super at low HP.
- **Additive fit:** launcher + juggles already approved; add WALL SPLAT
  (knock into walls/props = bonus + crumple) and Rage Art converges with
  TOP/Last Stand.
- **Effort:** M

### Combo breaker (read the combo) — *Killer Instinct* (2013)
- **Why players love it:** break a combo by matching its strength — defense
  becomes a read, not a guess, and counter-breakers punish the attempt.
- **Additive fit:** advanced: enemies can break YOUR combo if you repeat
  patterns (ties to anti-infinite). Teaches variety.
- **Effort:** M

### High damage + rage gauge + disarm — *Samurai Shodown* (1993+)
- **Why players love it:** one clean hit does 40% — every neutral exchange is
  terrifying, and the rage gauge rewards patience.
- **Additive fit:** boss design lesson: some bosses hit like trucks but telegraph
  hugely. High-stakes, fair fights. Design note for boss patterns.
- **Effort:** S (design note)

### Teching / DI / percentage knockback — *Super Smash Bros.* (1999+)
- **Why players love it:** directional influence and teching mean you're never
  truly helpless — survival is a skill, and every launch is interactive.
- **Additive fit:** launched players can TECH (tap on ground contact) to
  recover instantly with a small bounce. Never helpless.
- **Effort:** S

### Pattern-recognition bosses — *Punch-Out!!* (1987+)
- **Why players love it:** every boxer is a puzzle — learn the tell, punish
  the pattern. Beating Tyson is a memory palace, not a stat check.
- **Additive fit:** boss design law: every boss = readable patterns with
  unique tells (already have telegraphs — extend to full pattern kits).
  Design note for the boss worker.
- **Effort:** S (design note)

### Two-button purity — *Divekick* (2013)
- **Why players love it:** dive and kick. That's it. A parody that accidentally
  proved fundamentals > move count.
- **Additive fit:** philosophy guardrail: every new mechanic must survive the
  Divekick test — does it add depth or just buttons? Review criterion.
- **Effort:** — (principle)
