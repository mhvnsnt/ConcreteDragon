# CONCRETE DRAGON — ART DIRECTION

Owner-locked 2026-10-09. This is the visual law for the game, its promo art,
and every pipeline that produces art. Read it before making or approving anything visual.

## The mix

**LOOK = Nintendo. FEEL = classic fighters.**

- **Visual north stars:** Sonic (pop-art energy, speed, attitude), Zelda (painterly
  epic composition), Mario / Super Mario Sunshine (vibrant saturated color, subtle
  cel shading, "3D cartoon" joy), Sonic Adventure 2 (dynamic action poses, dramatic
  angles, characters bursting toward the viewer), Luigi's Mansion (moody-but-playful
  lighting, drama without losing charm).
- **Mechanics north stars:** the classic fighter/beat-em-up canon — Final Fight,
  Streets of Rage, Street Fighter, Tekken. Wave brawling, combos, juggles, grapples,
  boss fights. The *feel* is arcade fighter; the *look* is Nintendo.

## Key-art language (promo, banners, covers, ads)

Real game studios don't ship screenshots as key art — they paint it. Our promo art
follows the Nintendo key-art playbook:

1. **Dynamic action poses** — fighters mid-punch, mid-jump, mid-combo. Motion, not
   idle stances. Speed lines, impact bursts, dust kicks where it sells the hit.
2. **Bold, readable silhouettes** — every fighter recognizable at thumbnail size.
   Kid Blue's visor, the blocky toy-like builds, color-blocked per fighter identity.
3. **Saturated, joyful color** — the game's real palette: orange, teal, blue, pink,
   purple night tones. Sunshine-style pop, not grimdark.
4. **Dramatic composition** — diagonal energy, characters pushing toward the viewer,
   SA2-style. Foreground action, midground street, background skyline.
5. **Gloss and light** — cel-shaded highlights, neon glow, wet-street reflections.
   Luigi's-Mansion-style dramatic lighting when the scene calls for it.

## Grounded creativity (the hard rule)

Creative and imaginative is GOOD — but everything stays grounded in the real game:

- The game's **actual color palette**. No invented color worlds.
- **Things actually in the game** — or planned to be added, or that could be added.
  Real fighters, real streets, real objects (hydrants, boxes, taxis, lamps, benches).
- The graffiti "CONCRETE DRAGON" logo treatment (white or purple/gold) is the
  approved brand mark.
- **The line:** style the real game, don't invent a fake one. A dramatic painted
  Kid Blue uppercutting a Street Thug on our neon street = perfect. A photoreal
  dragon that isn't in the game = false advertising.

## In-game look (what the key art is selling)

- Blocky toy-like fighters, color-blocked per fighter (each fighter's colors stay
  tied to their identity and body areas).
- Colorful night-city streets: orange/teal/blue/pink buildings, purple ambient light.
- Clean readable HUD, chunky touch controls.
- The game must LOOK like its key art when you play it — same palette, same
  characters, same energy. If promo promises it, the build delivers it.

## Character design law (owner 2026-10-06, still standing)

- Keep the "different colored Lego pieces put together" color-blocked look.
- Genuine silhouette/personality differences: clothes, armor, mech/Gundam-like parts,
  vehicle-inspired parts, gis, jackets, gloves, headbands.
- Infinite unlockable characters; full parts/colors/textures customization.
- Cosmetics NEVER affect power. Every fighter: unique move set and combos.
- Every boss/minion/enemy archetype: distinct signature moves and behavior.
